# Research audit: IMU gait authentication

Reviewed on 23 October 2026. This is a focused audit of all 12 supplied PDFs, seven additional primary studies, and the model implementations in both model repositories. It is not an exhaustive literature review or a claim to reproduce the papers. The supplied PDFs were extracted with `pdftotext -layout` to the workspace directory `artifacts/research_audit_pdf_text/`; evidence locations below use one-based PDF pages. Public arXiv links were checked against their actual abstracts; the Lee et al. institutional PDF was also inspected.

## What the project can currently claim

The implemented authentication component is a convolutional IMU embedding baseline with metric learning and held-out-subject, within-recording verification. The classification models provide architectural comparisons, but activity accuracy does not measure identity verification. Current work includes authentic WISDM v1 participant IDs, segmented windowing, validation subjects excluded from optimizer training, walking filtering, activity-matched comparison sampling, empirical validation-FAR threshold selection, and CUDA support. These changes improve the experimental definition; they do not establish cross-session authentication or fidelity to every cited architecture. See [the verification protocol](../docs/verification_protocol.md).

The existing `STUDENT_B_SCOPE.md` and `B1_B3_reading_notes.md` / `B4_B7_reading_notes.md` explicitly describe an earlier, unverified reading-plan interpretation. Their statements that papers were unavailable, WISDM has no usable identities, or no validation split exists are historical and must not override current code and protocol documentation. This audit supplies paper-based corrections without editing those files.

## Evidence from the 12 supplied papers

“Adaptation” means a reusable idea was implemented with changed architecture, inputs, labels, or evaluation. A faithful reproduction would additionally require matching preprocessing, topology, objectives, dataset partitions, and operating-point definitions.

| Paper and evidence location | Actual modality and task | Reusable lesson | Fidelity of the current implementation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **B1 — Dehzangi et al., IMU-Based Gait Recognition Using Convolutional Neural Networks and Multi-Sensor Fusion** ([PDF][B1], p. 1 and method sections) | Accelerometer/gyroscope signals from five body locations; gait cycles expanded into **2D time-frequency representations**, followed by subject identification. | Compare sensor/location contributions and input-level versus decision-score fusion. | [MultiSensorFusionCNN](../models/cnn_baseline.py) processes each raw **1D channel** separately and fuses features. It has neither the paper's time-frequency pipeline nor its minimum-error score-fusion method. A generic fusion baseline, not a B1 reproduction; a sensor axis is not a separate body-worn IMU. |
| **B2 — Chen et al., METIER** ([PDF][B2], pp. 1, 4–9, 10) | Wearable time series; joint activity and user recognition, evaluated on SBHAR, UniMiB, and REALDISP. | Test whether activity and identity supervision help each other, with explicit ablations. | The paper uses task-specific networks with **soft parameter sharing and mutual attention**. [MultiTaskNet](../models/multitask.py) has one shared CNN–LSTM trunk, two linear heads, and weighted cross-entropies. This is a generic multi-task baseline, not METIER. A fixed user-class head also differs from verification of previously unseen users. |
| **B3 — Mekruksavanich and Jitpattanakul, Deep Learning Approaches for Continuous Authentication Based on Activity Patterns Using Mobile Sensing** ([PDF][B3], pp. 1, 5–8, 12–15) | Smartphone inertial activity patterns from UCI HAR, WISDM-HARB, and HMOG; the paper actually proposes DeepAuthen and reports genuine/impostor authentication by activity. | Keep the predicted target explicit and control the activity context; compare convolutional and recurrent features. | [DeepConvLSTM](../models/cnn_lstm.py) outputs **activity logits** in the local classification path. That run measures HAR, not direct gait verification. Conversely, calling the paper itself merely an activity-recognition study or a survey would also be inaccurate. Its architecture, preprocessing, and binary evaluation are not reproduced by sharing the DeepConvLSTM name. |
| **B4 — Sheng and Li, Siamese Denoising Autoencoders for Joints Trajectories Reconstruction and Robust Gait Recognition** ([PDF][B4], pp. 1, 3–6) | **Skeleton joint-coordinate trajectories**, including pose-estimation errors, with TUM GAID evaluation. The reconstruction target is a corrected skeleton trajectory. | Shared embeddings and corruption-aware reconstruction motivate robustness ablations. | [EmbeddingNet](../models/siamese.py) consumes IMU windows and trains a convolutional metric embedding. Its optional decoder is absent from the default embedding forward path and current metric objective. No skeleton estimation or joint-trajectory reconstruction is implemented. This is a cross-modality inspiration, not a Siamese DAE reproduction. |
| **B5 — Mekruksavanich and Jitpattanakul, Wearable Sensor-Based Behavioral User Authentication Using a Hybrid Deep Learning Approach with Squeeze-and-Excitation Mechanism** ([PDF][B5], pp. 1, 5–7, Fig. 2) | Inertial behavioral authentication on HMOG and USC-HAD, including walking and other activities. | Channel recalibration is a relevant IMU ablation; evaluate identity decisions rather than assume attention helps. | [SEConvNet](../models/attention.py) implements SE channel gates, but uses three convolutional stages, optional depthwise convolutions, and a flattened activity head. The paper depicts four blocks and global-average-pooled genuine/impostor classification. This is an SE adaptation; its reported authentication results are not reproduced. |
| **B6 — Ruiz-Barroso et al., Real-time Multiple People Gait Recognition in the Edge** ([PDF][B6], pp. 1, 4–5, Fig. 2) | **Video surveillance**: sets of 25 optical-flow maps of a person, processed by 2D/3D CNNs; deployment on Jetson Orin devices. | Measure accuracy, latency, throughput, and energy together; batching can affect decision latency. | [Compression utilities](../models/compress.py) supply pruning, quantization, and export for local models. They do not implement the video pipeline, TensorRT search, or Jetson energy experiment. A methodological deployment reference, not evidence of real-time IMU performance. |
| **B7 — Hrudya and Poornachandran, Efficient Gait Recognition with SEGait-ConViT** ([PDF][B7], pp. 1, 3, 7) | **Gait energy images derived from visual silhouettes**, combined with sparse Canny-edge information; CASIA-B and Gait3D. | Locality and patch representations can motivate a separately specified sensor-transformer comparison. | [GaitViT](../models/vit.py) uses 1D sensor patches and ordinary multi-head self-attention. It has no gait energy images, sparse-edge preprocessing, or paper-specific convolutional attention. Its class/module names do not establish SEGait-ConViT reproduction. |
| **C1 — Parashar et al., Journey into Gait Biometrics** ([PDF][C1], pp. 1–2 and review body) | A **review** of gait sensing and deep-learning pipelines, emphasizing real-time surveillance and visual representations. | Use its taxonomy to separate sensing modality, representation, network, and evaluation. | Background reading; no single experiment to reproduce. Its catalogued results are secondary evidence and cannot serve as IMU verification targets. |
| **C2 — Munusamy and Senthilkumar, Emerging Trends in Gait Recognition Based on Deep Learning: A Survey** ([PDF][C2], pp. 1, 8–10, 35) | A **survey** spanning appearance, skeleton, and sensor methods; many reviewed conditions concern camera viewpoint, clothing, and carrying. | Track nuisance conditions and return to the original study before adopting a benchmark figure. | Background and paper-discovery aid. Cross-view visual identification tables are not comparable to held-out-subject IMU pair ROC results. |
| **C3 — Zeng et al., Gait-Based Implicit Authentication Using Edge Computing and Deep Learning for Mobile Devices** ([PDF][C3], pp. 1, 7–10, 13) | Smartphone inertial signals transformed into **2D feature images**, then CNN–LSTM authentication; cloud training and edge inference. | Compare raw time-series and transformed representations under an identical verification protocol. | The project has a raw 1D CNN–LSTM and local inference utilities. It has not reproduced the image transform or EDIA cloud/edge system. Sensor provenance alone does not make the pipelines equivalent. |
| **C4 — Zou et al., Deep Learning-Based Gait Recognition Using Smartphones in the Wild** ([PDF][C4], pp. 1–4 and authentication experiments; [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1811.00338)) | Unconstrained smartphone accelerometer/gyroscope walking data; CNN/recurrent representations for identification and authentication. | Prioritize realistic walking collection and separate identification from authentication experiments. | Highly relevant motivation for inertial gait work; the current generic CNN–LSTM does not reproduce its datasets, architecture, or authentication classifier. Published accuracy is not a target EER for UCI HAR/WISDM. |
| **C5 — Alotaibi and Alotaibi, Hybrid Deep Learning Framework for Continuous User Authentication Based on Smartphone Sensors** ([PDF][C5], pp. 1, 8–10, 19) | MotionSense/UCI HAR inertial windows reframed for **subject identification**; patch extraction, multi-head attention, and BiLSTM. | Patch-plus-sequence modeling is relevant, but inspect target labels and split semantics before comparing. | The project has separate transformer and CNN–LSTM baselines, not this combined patch–attention–BiLSTM pipeline. Its subject-classification accuracy does not establish open-set verification, enrollment/probe separation, or continuous takeover detection. |

## Additional primary reading, ranked by usefulness

The applications below are proposed experiments, not claims that these methods are implemented or will improve this project's scores. These seven studies extend the supplied set; C4's arXiv version above is the same study as its bundled PDF.

1. **Gadaleta and Rossi, IDNet: Smartphone-based Gait Recognition with Convolutional Neural Networks** — [arXiv:1606.03238](https://arxiv.org/abs/1606.03238). The verified abstract describes orientation-independent walking-cycle extraction, CNN features, a one-class SVM, and aggregation across successive cycles. Read first for enrollment-specific decisions and temporal aggregation; the current pair scorer implements neither that SVM nor its multi-stage policy.
2. **Hoang, Choi, and Nguyen, On the Instability of Sensor Orientation in Gait Verification on Mobile Phone** — [arXiv:1602.03199](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.03199). The study explicitly challenges fixed phone orientation and proposes a compensation/recognition approach. It supports evaluating orientation shift; arbitrary rotations of normalized tensors are an experimental approximation, not a reproduction of its sensor-coordinate method.
3. **Dehghani, Glatard, and Shihab, Subject Cross Validation in Human Activity Recognition** — [arXiv:1904.02666](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.02666). It examines optimism from correlated samples and overlapping windows in HAR cross-validation. Preserve subject-disjoint representation training, but also separate genuine enrollment/probe recordings: subject separation alone does not eliminate dependence within test-subject windows.
4. **Lee et al., Gait-Based Continuous Authentication Using a Novel Sensor Compensation Algorithm and Geometric Features Extracted From Wearable Sensors** — [author-institution PDF](https://scholarworks.bwise.kr/hanyang/bitstream/2021.sw.hanyang/182135/1/Gait-Based_Continuous_Authentication_Using_a_Novel_Sensor_Compensation_Algorithm_and_Geometric_Features_Extracted_From_Wearable_Sensors.pdf). The abstract describes wrist/thigh recordings from 20 people across three days and different same-day/cross-day results. Its geometric/SVM pipeline differs from ours, but its recording design makes it a direct guide for session-shift evaluation.
5. **Fereidooni et al., AuthentiSense: A Scalable Behavioral Biometrics Authentication Scheme Using Few-Shot Learning for Mobile Platforms** — [arXiv:2302.02740](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02740). This is mobile interaction-motion authentication using a Siamese few-shot approach, with enrollment-shot and interaction-duration analyses. It motivates few-shot enrollment experiments; it is not specifically a walking-only gait benchmark.
6. **Cheung and Vhaduri, Continuous Authentication of Wearable Device Users from Heart Rate, Gait, and Breathing Data** — [arXiv:2008.10779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.10779). It proposes context-dependent authentication from several soft biometrics. The existing verification protocol's full-text review notes its small feasibility cohort and instance-level leave-one-out design; use it for activity availability and fallback policy, not unseen-subject performance comparisons.
7. **Delgado-Santos et al., GaitPrivacyON: Privacy-Preserving Mobile Gait Biometrics Using Unsupervised Learning** — [arXiv:2110.03967](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.03967). It combines a learned signal transformation with CNN/recurrent Siamese verification on MotionSense and MobiAct. Reserve this for a later privacy–utility experiment: identity discrimination alone does not measure whether sensitive attributes can be inferred from stored signals or embeddings.

The [official UCI HAR page](https://archive.ics.uci.edu/dataset/240/human+activity+recognition+using+smartphones) documents 30 participants, participant-level train/test partitioning, and overlapping windows. The [official Fordham WISDM release page](https://www.cis.fordham.edu/wisdm/dataset.php) distinguishes its releases and raw participant identifiers: WISDM v1 and the richer WISDM-HARB used in B3 are not interchangeable. The anonymous derived CSV in the project cannot recover identity from row order; the authentic `wisdm_v1` path is the appropriate identity-bearing source.

## Prioritized future experiments

All items below remain future work unless a separate experiment artifact demonstrates implementation. Existing walking filtering, paired sampling, validation subjects, and empirical threshold selection should be preserved as controls.

| Priority | Concrete experiment | Evidence and decision to report |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **P0: Independent enrollment and probe recordings** | Retain authentic recording/session IDs and raw sample offsets. Train the representation on one subject cohort, tune on another, then enroll unseen test subjects from an earlier recording and probe from later sessions; never share raw samples across enrollment/probe windows. | Lee et al., C4, and subject cross-validation motivate this design. Report session gap, genuine/impostor counts, per-subject errors, ROC/EER, and frozen-threshold FAR/FRR. If metadata is unavailable, retain the present within-recording label; a timestamp gap alone does not prove a new day/session. |
| **P0: Calibration and dependence** | Separate checkpoint selection from threshold calibration where cohort size permits. Freeze the attainable validation threshold before scoring test subjects; evaluate uncertainty by resampling independent subjects/recordings, preserving their dependent windows and comparisons. | The subject-dependence study and current protocol motivate this design. Report actual test FAR/FRR alongside EER, distinct subjects/recordings, unique/reused pairs, and the uncertainty method. Drawing more pairs from the same recordings does not create new independent evidence or guarantee a 1% deployment FAR. |
| **P1: Orientation and placement** | Compare the raw-vector baseline with magnitude channels, physically consistent rotation augmentation, and coordinate compensation when orientation sensors support it. Rotate corresponding physical vector groups coherently before train-fitted normalization; distinguish simulated rotation from a real placement-change recording. | Orientation-instability study, IDNet, and Lee et al. Report both unchanged-orientation and shifted-condition performance with identical partitions. Do not infer hardware-placement robustness from synthetic rotations alone. |
| **P1: Activity gating** | Add a subject-disjoint walking detector before verification, including nonwalking intervals, uncertain transitions, and a defined fallback or abstention. Evaluate the detector and end-to-end authentication together. | B2/B3 and Cheung–Vhaduri motivate context control. Ground-truth walking filtering is already implemented, but is not an online gate. Report walking coverage, gate false positives/negatives, and authentication availability, not only verification on oracle-selected walking. |
| **P1: Enrollment size and temporal decisions** | Compare one versus several nonoverlapping enrollment windows, then tune a simple mean-score or vote policy on chronologically ordered validation recordings. Freeze its thresholds and horizon before testing genuine streams and documented takeover scenarios. | IDNet and AuthentiSense motivate the comparison. Report elapsed observation time, false locks, time to detect takeover, and FAR/FRR. Ordered session metadata is a prerequisite; shuffled pair samples cannot establish detection delay. |
| **P2: Controlled architecture and deployment ablations** | After the protocol is fixed, compare a raw CNN embedding, CNN–LSTM, SE gates, and an IMU transformer under the same inputs, metric objective, partitions, and pair list. Treat time-frequency inputs or METIER-style mutual attention as separately specified larger experiments. | B1/B2/B5/C3/C5 supply architectural ideas; B6 supplies deployment methodology. Record parameters and actual target-device latency/energy separately from acquisition duration and network delay. CUDA support, zeroed weights, or successful ONNX export alone does not demonstrate a speed or energy improvement. |

## Existing measurements and reporting limits

The [frozen-code full-run report](../reports/2026-10-23/README.md) contains the canonical results and complete metrics. The earlier task handoff walking results below are separately archived preliminary runs, with EER expressed as a fraction:

| Archived run | Walking EER | ROC AUC | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| UCI HAR | 0.2044 | 0.8987 | [Darkstar HAR evaluation](../reports/2026-10-23/preliminary_integration/verification_har/evaluation/eval_verification.json) |
| Authentic WISDM v1 | 0.2170 | 0.8809 | [Darkstar WISDM evaluation](../reports/2026-10-23/preliminary_integration/wisdm_verification/evaluation/eval_verification.json) |

These are preliminary within-recording results for held-out subjects, not cross-session verification or a paper reproduction. Other run directories contain separate results and must be identified by their own configuration/checkpoint artifacts. The historical all-activity HAR EER of 0.4162 used a different activity/protocol scope and is not a controlled baseline for claiming a walking-model improvement. Likewise, neither AUC nor a validation-target FAR establishes the actual test decision error; report the JSON's frozen-threshold FAR and FRR as well.

For project writing, call the system **an IMU metric-learning baseline with paper-inspired classification and efficiency comparisons**. Read IDNet, the orientation study, Lee et al., and C4 first for the authentication protocol; B1/B2/B5 next for architectural ablations; C1/C2 for vocabulary and references; and B4/B6/B7 for transferable ideas with explicit modality caveats. No experimental gain is predicted by this audit.
